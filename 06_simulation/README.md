**Everything here is simulation output. Nothing was measured on hardware.**

| File | Purpose |

(≈ 6–8 CPU-hours on a 2-core machine; numpy ≥ 2, matplotlib).

## Model summary

* **Time:** rounds of 1 s (30 s in the city model). Each round the environment yields
  reception cells and interference domains.
* **Environments:** `auditorium` (one hall, 1 person/m², 10 m reception tiles re-drawn each round,
  35 m interference radius, slow walking); `rooms` (6 rooms × 500, room changes ~ every 20 min);
  `city` (Poisson encounters 1.5/h per person, 20 communities with 80 % in-community contacts,
  15 % group encounters of 3–8, mean contact 90 s); `barbell` (two rooms linked only by bridge
  devices).
* **Radio:** BLE-5 extended advertising; ADV_EXT_IND 136 µs on 3 primaries; 3-PDU AUX chains of
  1 064 µs on 37 secondaries; unslotted ALOHA success `e^{−2G}` on each; residual loss 10 %;
  receivers decode a chain prefix; host report ceiling 200/s; scan duty 30 % (5 % when alone).
* **Content:** each EXCHANGE/COURIER device authors one 1 KiB pamphlet (6 frames: manifest + 5
  chunks); fragments accumulate across events and senders; the manifest gates data frames.
* **Strategies:** BCAST (10 Hz, random), BCAST_RC (+ rate control), PAIRWISE (connections, blind
  push), AE_INV (connections, full inventory), HYBRID (rate control + least-recently-heard
  weighting + optional beacon-digest weighting + verified suppression + listener floor + burst mode
  for ≤ 8 transmitters), ORACLE (HYBRID's radio, perfect knowledge — an upper bound, not a design).
* **Adversaries:** `flood` (valid junk at 50 events/s ignoring rate control), `blackhole` (never
  forward), `suppress` (forged headers).
* **Energy:** radio-on seconds × nRF52840 datasheet currents (6.26 mA RX, 6.40 mA TX); CPU,
  flash and regulator excluded — comparative only.

Known simplifications: `08_verification/evidence_gaps.md` (bottom).

## Headline results (details: `results/summary.md`, `04_mathematical_analysis/convergence.md`)

1. **Rate control is necessary.** Fixed-rate broadcasting reaches 0 % at 3 000 devices per hall
   (group A). With rate control the hall still moves only ~300 kB/s: HYBRID reaches 95 % at 1 000
   devices but 40 % at 3 000 and 25 % at 5 000 within an hour.
2. **Only broadcast serves listeners.** Pairwise designs give LISTEN devices exactly 0 in every
   scenario.
3. **Overhearing beats beacons.** Least-recently-heard weighting is worth +8 points at N = 1 000;
   beacon digests +1.7 points there and −4 points at N = 3 000 (they eat event budget).
4. **Sparse contacts need burst mode.** City, 24 h: HYBRID 49 % without burst mode, 100 % with it
   (couriers and listeners), pairwise 100 % for couriers / 0 % for listeners.
5. **Rooms are the sweet spot:** 98 % in an hour across 6 × 500.
6. **Interdiction works:** silencing bridge couriers confines each side to its own half; a bridge
   that does not forward is no bridge.
7. **Flooding is jamming:** 10 % flooders → 86 %, 20 % → 29 % (N = 500 hall); no protocol fix.
8. **Storage floods:** 60/min admission quota best (0.82–0.88); time-stratified eviction does not
   help against continuous floods.
9. **Rate-control observability is the open risk:** counting only decodable neighbours collapses a
   1 000-device hall (0.47 vs 0.82).

## Honesty notes on this campaign

* Four model or code defects were found during the work and fixed, with the affected runs re-run:
  (i) a carousel change that re-sent each device's only item repeatedly (fixed by `I_REP`); (ii)
  pairwise baselines were not charged for flooders' traffic on shared data channels; (iii) the
  first time-stratified admission model treated a continuous flood as one epoch and reported
  100 % — wrong; (iv) a negative-count bug in stratified eviction (no visible effect, re-run anyway).
* Results in groups A–G for HYBRID use beacons **on** (the original configuration); group H re-runs
  the specified v1 configuration (beacons off). Where they differ it is stated.
* Group C "rooms" was shortened from 2 h to 1 h and to one seed for non-HYBRID strategies to fit
  the compute budget.
